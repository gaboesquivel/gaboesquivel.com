USDT is one of the main ways value moves through [DeFi](/web3), and moving it between chains is still the weak point, because traditional bridges add security risk, fragment liquidity, and make users pick routes by hand.

Many bridges rely on wrapped assets like wUSDT or USDT.e, which can lose their peg or run short of liquidity, and others depend on centralized multisig custodians, which have been prime targets for hacks such as the $600M Ronin Bridge attack and the $320M Wormhole exploit. USDT0, built on LayerZero's Omnichain Fungible Token (OFT) standard, addresses those problems with a more direct way to move USDT across blockchains.

## How USDT0 moves between chains

USDT0 is a bridged version of USDT that moves across chains without wrapping or fragmenting liquidity. Instead of relying on external liquidity providers, it uses lock-and-mint:

1. USDT is locked on [Ethereum](/tech/ethereum).
2. USDT0 is minted 1:1 on the destination chain.
3. When USDT0 is sent back, it is burned and the USDT is unlocked.

Unlike versions Tether issues directly, such as native USDT on Solana or Tron, USDT0 is managed by Everdawn Labs and moves through LayerZero's OFT standard.

## What changes for users and protocols

Compared with wrapped USDT and pool-based bridges, USDT0 changes five things:

- Transfers cross chains without wrapping or splitting liquidity.
- Validation runs through decentralized mechanisms instead of a single custodian.
- The token stays at peg because every USDT0 is backed 1:1 by locked USDT.
- Liquidity is unified instead of split into pools on each chain.
- Users no longer pick a bridge route by hand.

## USDT0 compared with traditional bridges

| Feature | USDT0 (LayerZero OFT) | Traditional Bridges |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Transfer Mechanism | Lock-and-mint | Liquidity pools & wrapping |
| Standard Used | LayerZero OFT | Custom bridge protocols |
| Issuer | Everdawn Labs | Tether (for native issuance) / third-party bridges |
| Native or Wrapped? | Native USDT | Often wrapped (wUSDT, USDT.e) |
| Security Model | Decentralized (oracles & relayers) | Multi-sig / validator-based (higher risk) |
| Liquidity | Unified across chains | Fragmented across chains |
| Tradability | Needs conversion tool | Often tradable but may depeg |

## Where the security gain comes from

Security has been the largest problem with traditional bridges, and hacks against centralized custodians have cost billions.

USDT0 has no liquidity pools to drain, so pool exploits and pool-driven depegs are off the table. It relies on LayerZero's decentralized oracles and relayers instead of a single point of failure, and without multisig wallets or external custodians in the path, the attack surface is much smaller while USDT keeps its stability on every chain it reaches.

The remaining risk sits with LayerZero's verification and with the teams operating USDT0, and I expect adoption to grow as more of DeFi moves away from wrapped stablecoins toward paths like this one.
