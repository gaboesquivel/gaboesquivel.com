# Channels

Use the requested audience, format, and length. The guidance below supplies defaults, not a mandatory structure. Destination repo docs own site routes, page jobs, and package fields.

## Website, hero, CTA, and metadata

Make the product focus or relevant capability understandable first. Add proof where it helps; every short field need not carry a shipped fact. Give a CTA a clear action and destination appropriate to the page. Use the booking destination in the identity reference when the intended action is booking a call.

## Biography and LinkedIn About

For a biography, establish who Gabo is and why his experience matters to this audience. An About section can develop a project or judgment with relevant experience. Use a third-person bio when requested; first person usually fits an About section. Select supporting projects rather than inventorying the stack. Let the chosen focus determine which details belong. Give LinkedIn About enough space to develop that view; it is not an X post.

## Blog and technical explanations

Build an argument the reader can follow. Ground unfamiliar concepts before relying on them; explain acronyms when the audience needs it. Use examples and implementation detail in proportion to the topic. Technical architecture writing should preserve boundaries, dependencies, failure modes, and uncertainty supported by the source.

Use descriptive headings and paragraphs shaped by the argument. Default to title case for post titles and sentence case for section headings, subject to the destination's conventions. Explain what code examples demonstrate. Lists suit parallel items; prose suits a developing argument.

## Product announcements

Lead with what people can now do and why it matters, then explain Gabo's contribution and relevant technical choices. Distinguish shipped capability from planned work. Use availability, dates, metrics, quotes, and calls to try a product only when supplied or verified. A retrospective draft must not imply a new release today.

## LinkedIn posts and X

When sharing a source piece, read it and select a useful angle the piece already supports. Keep factual claims within the source; make its point understandable through faithful paraphrase. LinkedIn can develop the idea with context; X normally needs a single compact point. When both are requested, provide separately adapted versions unless a shared draft is requested.

For a standard short X post, default to at most 280 characters including the supplied link; honor an explicit tighter limit. Keep supplied URLs, including short links, unchanged. Identify a source hero image separately when sharing the article and one is available; do not invent an image or create a short link.

## Outreach

Connect the reason for reaching out to the recipient's known context and make a proportionate ask. Include experience, engagement terms, or the calendar link when relevant to a hiring or collaboration conversation. A peer exchange, thank-you, or follow-up need not become a sales pitch.

## Spoken or voice-agent

Use conversational sentences that are easy to say and follow. Two to four sentences often suit a short answer; adapt to the requested duration and substance. Render a spoken script without visual formatting or raw URLs unless the task calls for them.
